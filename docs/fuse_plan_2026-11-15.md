# Fuse plan: from the pack to the Pi

Status: recommendation, 2026-11-15. Kevin decides and buys. Nothing is
ordered, and nothing on the robot is changed. A Grok second opinion is in
section 8. UPDATE 2026-11-15 (later the same day): Kevin's answers are in
this issue (sections 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7). The main switch is a Flipsky
Anti-spark Switch Pro V3.0 in the NEGATIVE lead (photo:
`docs/data/power_20261115/flipsky_antispark_switch_20261115.jpg`).

Scope: the main pack, the BMS, the main switch, the buck converter (the
"48 V to 12 V converter"), the Geekworm X1202, the Pi 5 and the camera
feed. The VESC settings do not change in this plan.

Sources: `docs/battery_settings.md`, `docs/pi_power_architecture.md`
(section 7), `docs/power_module_spec.md` (3.1, 3.2, 3.2a, 3.12),
`docs/x1202_power_watcher_design.md`, `docs/ups_hat_b.md`,

## 1. Summary

Fit these fuses, all in positive leads:

1. **FM, the main fuse.** 40 A at the pack positive. A 40 A fuse is
   already on the battery (Kevin). Read the DC rating on it. 32 V (an
   automotive blade, MIDI or generic ANL): replace it now. 58 V:
   acceptable, replace it at the next service. 80 V or more: keep it.
   The replacement is an Eaton Bussmann ANL-40 (80 V DC, 2.7 kA at
   80 V DC) in a Bussmann 4164 block, with a cover.
2. **FB-IN, the buck input.** 5 A where the buck + input lead joins the
   main positive, after FM. This lead is live with the main switch off,
   because the switch is in the negative lead. Littelfuse KLKD005
   (10 x 38 mm, 600 V DC, 50 kA DC) in a Bussmann HEB-AA holder with two
   2A0660 boots.
3. **FB-OUT, the buck output.** 5 A at the + output terminal of the buck,
   on the lead that ends in the X1202 barrel plug. The same KLKD005 and
   HEB-AA.
4. **FC, the charge lead.** 10 A on the + wire of the charge lead, at the
   pack end. Necessary: the BMS has a separate charge lead (Kevin), and
   the charge port is live. Littelfuse KLKD010 in an HEB-AA.
5. **F5V, the 5 V jumpers.** 5 A in each jumper from the X1202 "5V Out"
   sockets, at the X1202 end. Littelfuse 0997005.WXN in a Littelfuse
   0FHM0002ZXJM holder. This is in the harness, not on the Pi shield.

Not necessary: a fuse on a camera 5 V wire. The OAK-D Lite and the
Arduino are on USB ports of the Pi, and there is no hub (Kevin).

Do not fuse a negative lead. Do not fit a fuse in each VESC lead (4.3).

A fuse does not stop an overvoltage. The adjustable buck can put more
than 18 V on the X1202 (a turned trimmer, or a failed buck). Section 6
item 2 gives the fix: a fixed 12 V converter with an input rating of
90 V or more.

Notes:

- The HEB-AA crimps take 16 AWG or heavier. Crimp a 16 AWG pigtail into
  the holder, then splice to a thinner lead.
- The Eaton boot listing says: with boots, size a fuse at 200 percent of
  the RMS load or more. FB-IN is at 500 percent, FC at 250 percent, FB-OUT at 210 percent
  of the 2.4 A charge load. If FB-OUT opens with no fault, go to 7 A and a
  20 AWG lead.
- If the cell datasheet shows a pack resistance below 20 mOhm, the short
  current can pass 2.7 kA. Then use a Class T 40 A fuse for FM (for
  example Littelfuse JLLN 40 A; VERIFY its DC rating and holder).

## 4. Checks

### 4.1 DC voltage rating

- The pack reaches 54.6 V at full charge. The BMS pack OVP trip is
  55.3 V.
- Each fuse that can see the pack has a DC rating above that, with
  margin: ANL-40 80 V DC, KLKD 600 V DC.
- The 58 V MINI and MIDI fuses are not used on the pack side. Their
  interrupt rating is 1 kA, and 58 V is only 2.7 V above the OVP trip.
- F5V is on the 5 V rail. Any DC rating is sufficient there.

### 4.2 Fault current and interrupt rating

- A fuse must break the largest current that a short at its terminals can
  cause. Fault current = pack voltage / resistance up to the short.
- With the measured 42 mOhm (which includes some wiring): 54.6 V / 42
  mOhm = 1.3 kA. A short at the FM studs sees less resistance, thus more
  current.
- ANL-40: 2.7 kA at 80 V DC. KLKD: 50 kA DC. Both are sufficient unless
  the pack resistance is below 20 mOhm (section 3, notes).
- The BMS short-circuit trip (280 A) usually acts first. Do not depend on
  it: a BMS FET that fails short gives no trip.

### 4.3 Wire gauge against the fuse

The fuse protects the wire. Each wire must carry the fuse rating. Values
for one copper wire in free air, short run (chassis wiring). In a bundle
or in a hot enclosure, use less.

| Fuse | Rating | Minimum wire |
|---|---|---|
| FM, and each VESC lead | 40 A | 12 AWG is in place (41 A). Acceptable with silicone or 105 C insulation, short runs, not in a bundle. If a main wire is hot to the touch after a long climb, go to 10 AWG |
| FC | 10 A | 18 AWG (16 A); 12 AWG is in place |
| FB-IN | 5 A | 22 AWG (7 A); 16 AWG pigtail into the HEB |
| FB-OUT | 5 A | 22 AWG; 20 AWG preferred |
| F5V | 5 A | 22 AWG (the maximum for an XH crimp); 150 mm or less |

- The VESC leads are 12 AWG, the same as the main path, so FM protects
  them. Do not fit a fuse in one VESC lead: if it opens, that side stops
  and the other side drives on, and the robot turns (Grok, item 12).
- Do not fuse a negative lead. If a negative fuse opens, the return
  current finds a path through the CAN, USB and signal grounds.
- The main switch is in the negative lead. Thus each positive wire is
  live also with the switch off. A positive wire that touches the
  switched negative or a metal part still makes a short with the switch
  off. FM, FB-IN and FC are the protection for that.

### 4.4 Location: at the source end

- Put each fuse at the end of the wire that connects to the larger
  source. A short anywhere along the wire is then downstream of the fuse.
- FM: at the pack, within 150 mm. FB-IN: where the buck + lead leaves the
  main positive. FC: where the charge + lead leaves the pack. FB-OUT: at
  the buck output. F5V: at the X1202.
- Connect the buck + to the main positive through FB-IN. Connect the
  buck - to the switched negative (the switch OUT - or a negative bus),
  not to a VESC terminal.
- Do not connect the system negative (the buck -, the Pi, CAN, USB) to
  B- or to the BMS P- on the battery side of the switch. That connection
  goes around the switch, and the switch then does not turn the robot
  off.
- Connect the charge lead at one point only. If it joins on both sides of
  FM or of the main switch, it is a 10 A bypass around them.
- The `docs/power_module_spec.md` rules are the same: its F1 (converter
  input at the switched-side end, 80 V DC or more) is FB-IN here, and its
  F2 (12 V lead, rated for pack voltage) is FB-OUT here. Its F3 and F4
  (battery tap, ignition lead) are not used by option F.

### 4.5 The buck: inrush and output current

- Output: the X1202 took 0.89 A at 12.2 V with the Pi idle and the camera
  on, and 2.2 to 2.4 A with a cell charge. With a full CPU load, estimate
  34 W, thus about 2.8 A. The buck must give 3 A or more without a stop;
  5 A is better (`docs/pi_power_architecture.md` 7.10, item 7).
- Input: 34 W at 88 percent from 40 V is about 1.0 A; from 54.6 V about
  0.7 A.
- Inrush: when the main switch closes, the buck input capacitors charge.
  For 200 uF at 54.6 V the energy is about 0.3 J, which is small against
  a 5 A fuse. VERIFY at the first switch-on. If FB-IN opens with no fault,
  the buck has large input capacitors: go to 7 A.
- A buck makes more current out than it takes in: 10 A out at 12 V is
  only about 2.5 A in at 50 V. Thus FB-IN cannot protect the 12 V lead,
  and FB-OUT is necessary.

### 4.6 The X1202 6 V to 18 V input

- Set the buck to about 12.2 V before the X1202 uses it. The X1202 limit
  is 18 V.
- A fuse cannot protect the X1202 from overvoltage. If the buck fails
  with its high-side FET shorted, 54 V to 90 V reaches the X1202 while
  the current is still a few amperes. FB-OUT holds, the X1202 fails, and
  the cells can be damaged. A trimmer that moves (vibration) is the same
  type of fault.
- The fix is a converter change, not a fuse (section 6, item 2).
- The 12 V goes into the barrel jack, held by a cable tie or a
  right-angle plug (Kevin, 2026-11-15). WARNING: never make a 12 V lead
  with an XH plug. All 2-pin sockets on the X1202 are XH2.54, and a 12 V
  XH plug fits a 5 V socket.

### 4.7 Regen and the main fuse

- A fuse opens in both directions. Regen is -5 A on each VESC (-10 A in
  total, part A, 2026-11-11). It cannot open a 40 A fuse.
- If FM opens while the robot brakes, the VESCs lose the pack as a load
  for the regen current. The bus capacitors then charge: at 10 A into
  about 2 mF (VERIFY), about 5 V/ms. The bus reaches the VESC fault
  (`l_max_vin` 90 V) in about 7 ms. The VESCs then release the motors,
  and the robot coasts with no power.
- This is the same event as a main switch opened while the robot moves,
  or a BMS discharge-FET trip. It is worse than a BMS charge-FET trip,
  because with only the charge FET open the drive current still pulls
  the bus down.
- Each part across the bus sees up to 90 V in that event. That
  includes the buck. A buck with a 60 V or 75 V input maximum can fail
  there, and its failure puts pack voltage on the X1202 (4.6).
- The voltage across the opening fuse is the bus minus the pack: up to
  about 90 V - 40 V = 50 V. That is inside the 80 V rating of the ANL-40.
- The Flipsky switch is rated 60 V (14S). In the same event (FM or the
  BMS discharge FET opens while the robot brakes), the switch can also
  see up to 90 V. The fix is the same as for the buck: the VESC
  overvoltage fault (`l_max_vin`, `docs/power_module_spec.md` open
  question 22). That is Kevin's decision, not a fuse.
- No fuse prevents regen overvoltage. The regen cutoff ramp, `l_max_vin`
  and the dump clamp of `docs/power_module_spec.md` 3.2a are the controls
  for it.
- Do not use a fuse rating that can open in normal driving. 40 A keeps a
  margin above the 30 A drive total. If FM ever opens with no fault, go to
  ANL-50 on 8 AWG, not lower.

### 4.8 What the BMS covers

The JHB-R24TH covers the main path as an electronic switch. It does not
replace the fuses.

| Fault | BMS | Fuse |
|---|---|---|
| Overload above 40 A | Opens after its OCP delay | FM is the backup |
| Hard short (above 280 A) | Opens in microseconds, usually first | FM is the backup |
| A BMS FET that fails short (the usual BMS failure) | Nothing | FM |
| A thin branch wire that overheats at 7 A to 39 A | Nothing: under the 40 A OCP | FB-IN, FB-OUT, FC |
| The cell-side wiring (B+ to the BMS) | Nothing | Nothing. FM is as near the cells as the pack allows |
| Overvoltage on the X1202 | Nothing | Nothing (4.6) |

Keep "Emergency force start" OFF in the BMS (`docs/battery_settings.md`).

### 4.9 The 5 V jumpers (not on the Pi shield)

The Pi shield has no fuse on its 5 V input (decided). The jumpers come
from the X1202 "5V Out" XH sockets: one jumper now to the top HAT, two to
the shield later. Recommendation: one 5 A fuse in each jumper, at the
X1202 end (F5V).

Why:

- The X1202 does not publish a short-circuit limit for its 5 V output.
  With the input off, the 5 V comes from two Molicel IHR-18650C high-rate
  cells. If the 5 V stage cannot limit a short, the cells can feed tens of
  amperes into a 22 AWG jumper.
- The jumpers are the most exposed 5 V wires: screw terminals, a harness
  that moves, metal near them.
- The cost is small. A 5 A MINI fuse is about 15 to 20 mOhm. At 3.3 A
  busy the Pi rail loses about 25 mV to 35 mV. With the jumper the rail
  was 5.10 V busy (minimum 5.06 V); the limit is 4.9 V.

How:

- One fuse for each jumper, not one fuse for both. One shared fuse would
  put the full current through one XH pin (3 A rating).
- After the fit, run the load test again: 4 busy cores and an NVMe read.
  Pass: EXT5V 5.0 V or more and `get_throttled` 0x0.
- Keep each jumper at 150 mm or less, in a sleeve, with a strain relief,
  away from the pack and 12 V wires.
- Do not use a PTC here. A PTC adds 50 mOhm to 250 mOhm, which is the
  drop that the jumper removes.

The fuse does not cover a short on the Pi side of the pogo pins. The pogo
path (0.2 ohm) limits that current.

## 5. Not covered by this plan

- The CAN, USB and signal wiring.
- The Arduino and receiver 5 V, and the RTK rover 5 V: the shield has
  its own PTCs (MF-R030) for them.
- The motor phase leads. The VESC limits them.

## 6. Items that are not fuses

1. **The main switch.** ANSWERED 2026-11-15: a Flipsky Anti-spark Switch
   Pro V3.0 in the negative lead, 14S / 60 V maximum, 60 A continuous,
   800 A peak. It is an electronic switch with its own soft start, so it
   is the precharge, and it does not arc. Its 60 A is above the 40 A of
   FM. Two limits stay. Its 60 V is below the 90 V bus pump (4.7). An
   electronic switch can fail closed (shorted FETs); it then does not
   turn the robot off, and no fuse helps. Check it at each start: switch
   off, and the VESC LEDs must go dark.
2. **The buck.** Replace the adjustable buck with a fixed 12 V converter
   (12 V to 13.8 V out): input range 40 V to 55 V or wider, input maximum
   90 V or more (the VESC fault), 3 A or more out (5 A better), output
   overvoltage protection. The 72 V-class golf-cart converters of
   `docs/power_module_spec.md` 3.2 have this input range. Until then:
   lock the trimmer with paint after the 12.2 V setting. If the buck
   cannot change, an overvoltage crowbar at the X1202 input that shorts
   the 12 V lead and opens FB-OUT is the fuse-compatible fix.
3. **The old USB hub supply.** The hub is gone (Kevin). If its supply
   from the pack is still wired, remove it, or fuse it at the pack end
   for its wire.
4. **The FM block, if the ANL-40 replaces the fuse on the battery.** The
   4164 has open studs. Put it in a closed, dry box or under an
   insulating cover, out of any water path. Torque the studs, and
   strain-relieve both cables. Order two boots (2A0660) for each HEB-AA;
   the holder comes without them.
